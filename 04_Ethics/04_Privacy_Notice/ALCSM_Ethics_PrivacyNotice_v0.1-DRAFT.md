## What is this study about?

The study looks at the credentials people publicly present when they hold a job title implying AI leadership (for example, "Chief AI Officer" or "Head of AI") on LinkedIn, and compares those presented credentials with what a separate sample of job postings for similar roles say they require. **The study measures what is publicly shown, not a person's real ability or performance.** No individual is named, judged, or contacted as part of this research, and no individual-level result is ever published.

## Why am I receiving this notice?

If your publicly visible LinkedIn profile presents a title matching this study's research criteria, it may have been included in a sample of approximately 500 profiles reviewed as part of this research. Because it is not practical to contact every individual in a sample of this size directly, this public notice is the way this study meets its transparency obligation to you under data protection law (specifically, Article 14 of the EU General Data Protection Regulation).

## What information was recorded?

Only the text content of your profile's sections (e.g. headline, about, experience, education, certifications) that was **publicly visible at the time of review**, plus a small number of publicly observable indicators (such as your approximate network size or the number of posts you have made, if that was public). Specifically, **this study does not record and does not store**:

- your name,
- your profile URL or web address,
- any photograph,
- your email address,
- your phone number.

Your record in this study's database is identified only by a randomly assigned code (e.g. `PRF-00042`), not by any of the above.

## What is the legal basis for this?

Because contacting each of the ~500 individuals in the sample individually is not practical and would itself distort the study (only people who chose to respond would remain in the data), this study relies on **legitimate interests** (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR) as its legal basis, following a documented assessment weighing the research interest against your rights as an individual. A copy of that assessment's reasoning is available on request (see contact details below).

Under GDPR, you have the right to:

- ask whether your data is included and to see it (**right of access**);
- ask for it to be corrected if inaccurate (**right of rectification**);
- ask for it to be deleted (**right of erasure**) — please note that because records are pseudonymised, locating a specific individual's record may not always be possible;
