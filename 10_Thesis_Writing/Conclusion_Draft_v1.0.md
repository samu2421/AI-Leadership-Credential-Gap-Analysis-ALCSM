# Chapter 6: Conclusion (Draft v1.0)

*Draft. Synthesizes Chapters 4-5; introduces no new results.*

---

This thesis set out to audit observable AI leadership credential signals across 500 LinkedIn profiles using a six-dimensional formative scoring framework (ALCSM), to compare those signals against 201 job postings' stated requirements, to assess the framework's measurement reproducibility through expert content-assessment and independent inter-rater reliability evidence, to examine whether observable professional-impact data could validate the framework against external criteria, and to test whether a minimal, local NLP baseline could approximate the human-coded scores.

The credential-signal patterns observed across the 500 profiles (RQ1) show six genuinely distinct dimensions rather than a single underlying trait — different score distributions, different missingness rates, and different degrees of reproducibility, consistent with ALCSM's formative design. The job-posting comparison (RQ2) shows that demand and supply signals are not uniform across dimensions, and that job-posting text as a data source is poorly suited to capturing some credential signals (D6 in particular) regardless of how observable those signals are on the profile side. The measurement-reproducibility evidence (RQ3) is the thesis's central methodological contribution: two dimensions (D1, D6) show strong, well-supported reproducibility; two (D2, D4) show perfect but statistically uninformative agreement; and two (D3, D5) show specific, diagnosed reliability limitations rather than an undifferentiated "unreliable" verdict — a systematic rater-calibration gap for D3, and range restriction combined with a narrower boundary ambiguity for D5. RQ4 remains genuinely open, owing to a data-collection gap rather than a negative finding. The NLP baseline (RQ5) — a proof-of-concept, not a production or deployment-ready system — shows modest, dimension-dependent signal: genuine positive signal above a majority-class baseline for D1 and D3, and no advantage over that baseline for D2, D4, D5, or D6, with the D3 result — strongest NLP signal, weakest human reliability — standing out as a finding worth further investigation rather than a resolved contradiction.

**ALCSM is not presented, at the end of this thesis, as a fully validated instrument.** It is presented as a formative credential-signal framework with dimension-specific, evidence-backed strengths and limitations, each traceable to a specific, reported analysis rather than asserted. This is offered as a more defensible and more useful contribution than a single overall validity claim would have been, given the scope achievable within an MSc thesis: it tells a future user of ALCSM, or a future extension of this work, precisely which dimensions can currently be trusted for what purpose, and precisely what would need to change — clearer rulebook wording for D3, a more carefully operationalized leadership-scope criterion for D5, new criterion data for RQ4, a richer feature representation for the NLP baseline's weaker dimensions — to strengthen the framework further.

No composite ALCSM score, weighting scheme, or classification band was introduced at any point in this thesis, consistent with the position, stated throughout, that combining six non-interchangeable formative indicators into a single number is a decision requiring its own dedicated empirical justification that this thesis's scope did not extend to providing.
