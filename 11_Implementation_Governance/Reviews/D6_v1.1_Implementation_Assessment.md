# D6 v1.1 Implementation Assessment

**Purpose:** Consolidated assessment of the proposed D6 replacement (Professional Engagement & Continuous Development → AI Industry/Application Breadth), covering empirical evidence, the candidate rubric, D3-overlap analysis, job-posting applicability, database impact, affected documents, pilot design, risks, and next steps.
**Status:** PROPOSED / PILOT_REQUIRED — see `DECISION_LOG.md` D-0038. This report does not authorize annotation, NLP implementation, or a schema migration.
**Author:** Samiksha Dhere (researcher) — drafted with Claude Code assistance
**Date:** 2026-08-17

---

## 1. Summary

The original D6 (Professional Engagement and Continuous Development) cannot be operationalized against the collected dataset: zero of 500 included profiles have any recorded post/activity evidence, and the ethics justification its collection would require was never drafted. The author has replaced the construct — retaining a six-dimension model — with **AI Industry/Application Breadth**, defined as *"the breadth of distinct industries or application domains in which an individual has observable AI-related professional experience."* This report assembles the empirical evidence, candidate rubric, distinctiveness analysis, job-posting applicability, schema impact, and pilot design needed to move from proposal to a pilot-tested, author-frozen v1.1 dimension. **Recommendation: proceed to pilot; do not freeze until pilot results are reviewed.**

## 2. Empirical evidence (recomputed against real data)

All figures recomputed directly from `ALCSM_LinkedIn_Processed_v1.1.xlsx` (`Professional_Experience` sheet) and `ALCSM_JobPosting_Processed_v1.1.xlsx` (`Requirements` sheet) for this task — none carried forward from an earlier feasibility estimate, per explicit instruction.

- **Coverage:** 494/500 profiles (98.8%) have ≥1 AI-related experience record (`AI_Related = Yes`) with a usable `Industry` value. Of the 1,791 AI-related experience rows, 1,740 (97.2%) carry a usable industry value; the remaining 51 carry the non-informative placeholder `Other`.
- **Restriction matters empirically, not just definitionally:** recomputing breadth over all experience (AI-related and not) rather than AI-related-only inflates the result for 55/495 profiles (11%) and produces one nonsensical 14-industry outlier — confirming the AI-related filter is load-bearing, not cosmetic.
- **Distribution** (full 500-profile set, after normalization): 0→6 (1.2%), 1→239 (47.8%), 2→155 (31.0%), 3→67 (13.4%), 4→28 (5.6%), 5→5 (1.0%). Mean ≈1.77, median 2, max observed 5 (no artificial ceiling needed).
- **Banding feasibility:** the full 0–5 range is populated without severe compression; the main disclosed concern is concentration in bands 1–2 (78.8% combined) and thin tails at 0 and 5 — normal for a low-ceiling count measure, not a measurement failure, and stated as a limitation rather than minimized.
- **Full method, normalization table, and per-question detail:** `ALCSM_D6_Industry_Breadth_v1.1.md` §5–§7.

## 3. Proposed rubric

Direct count-to-band mapping (score = min(distinct normalized AI-related industries, 5)), matching the observed distribution with no compression transform:

| Score | Criterion | n | % |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | No AI-related experience with identifiable industry context | 6 | 1.2% |
| 1 | Exactly one distinct industry/domain | 239 | 47.8% |
| 2 | Exactly two | 155 | 31.0% |
| 3 | Exactly three | 67 | 13.4% |
| 4 | Exactly four | 28 | 5.6% |
| 5 | Five or more | 5 | 1.0% |

Candidate only; not binding until pilot-confirmed (`ALCSM_D6_Industry_Breadth_v1.1.md` §7).

## 4. D3 overlap analysis

**Structural:** D3's frozen taxonomy (`ALCSM_Measurement_Specification_v1.0.md` §2.3) and scoring criteria (`ALCSM_v1.0_Scoring_Framework_Task3.md` §1) are built entirely from years-in-AI/ML-role and ownership-language strength. "Industry sector of AI application" appears once, in the original Task 2 design notes, as a *measurable indicator worth recording* — never as a scored criterion, and it is absent from the frozen spec, rulebook, and annotation handbook. No existing D3 field measures industry count or diversity.

**Empirical (proxy-based, since real D3 scores do not yet exist):**

| Proxy pairing | Statistic | Value | Shared variance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Industry breadth vs. total AI-related tenure (months) | Pearson r | 0.240 | ≈5.8% |
| Industry breadth vs. total AI-related tenure (months) | Spearman ρ | 0.453 | — |
| Industry breadth vs. count of AI-related roles held | Pearson r | 0.633 | ≈40.1% |

The tenure-proxy correlation is weak-to-moderate; the role-count correlation is stronger but mechanical (holding more roles creates more chances to touch a new industry) and role count is itself not a D3 scoring criterion. Cross-tabulation against duration tertiles shows a monotonic but incomplete relationship (e.g., 47.7% of low-tenure profiles are breadth=1 vs. only 18.6% of high-tenure profiles; conversely, 80% of breadth=5 profiles are high-tenure) — consistent with the intended framing: **D3 = depth, D6 = breadth**, correlated because both draw on the same underlying `Professional_Experience` rows, but not redundant. This correlation is reported strictly for non-redundancy purposes and must never be read as, or reported alongside, a Cronbach's-alpha-style internal-consistency claim — ALCSM's dimensions are formative, not reflective (`ALCSM_Measurement_Model_and_Validation_Note.md` §1).

## 5. Job-posting applicability and requirement-mapping impact (Task 6 / Task 11)

| Category | Count |

| (B) Single-domain specificity (opposite of breadth) | 9 |
| (C) Mentions industry/domain/sector generically, not breadth-relevant | 11 |
| (D) Not relevant (no industry/domain/sector language) | 1,536 |
| (E) Unclear (ambiguous either/or sector list) | 1 |

**Mapping impact under v1.1, relative to the current `Final_ALCSM_Dimension` field:**

| Status under new D6 | Count | Note |
|---|---|---|
| D6-mappable (clean candidate) | 1 | The "across multiple domains" strategy requirement; currently mapped to D5 — a genuine D5/D6 boundary case, tie-breaking rule proposed in `ALCSM_Coding_Rulebook_v1.1_Addendum.md` §9, not resolved here. |
| D6-not-applicable (industry-relevant but not a breadth signal) | 20 | The (B) and (C) rows — single-domain depth requirements and generic domain/sector mentions. |
| D6-unclear | 1 | The (E) row. |
| Not Directly Mapped (no industry signal) | 1,536 | Unchanged status. |
| **Currently D6-mapped, now orphaned** | **7** | All 7 requirements presently mapped to the retired "Professional Engagement" D6 (thought-leadership/community/continuing-education language) contain no industry/domain language whatsoever and do not map to D6 under any reading of the new definition. These require re-triage (most plausibly to "Not Directly Mapped," possibly partially to D5 for the thought-leadership-specific ones) — **not performed in this task**; no change has been made to `ALCSM_JobPosting_Processed_v1.1.xlsx`. |

**Reported finding, not corrected:** D6 v1.1 is observable on 494/500 profiles (98.8%) but explicitly required in essentially none of 1,558 job postings (1, 0.06%). This asymmetry is legitimate to report as-is — employers in this corpus almost never state a cross-industry AI-experience requirement, they far more often (when they mention industry at all) ask for depth in one specific vertical, which is the structural opposite of what D6 measures. No single-industry requirement has been reinterpreted as a breadth signal, and the six-dimension model has not been restructured to force symmetry between the two corpora.

**Incidental finding, out of scope for this task, reported not fixed:** while sampling profile IDs for the pilot (§7), the real dataset's `Profile_ID` values use the format `LP####` (e.g., `LP0001`…`LP0611`), confirmed as the dataset's own valid convention in `P3_Final_Data_QA_Report.md` ("Profile_ID format conformance (LP####): 611/611 conform"). This is a pre-existing load-time incompatibility, unrelated to D6 and not introduced or worsened by this task; it is noted here only because it surfaced incidentally, and is left for a separate, author-directed data-loading/ID-mapping review rather than addressed as part of this D6 revision.

## 7. Pilot design

**Sample:** 18 profiles (within the requested 15–20 range), purposively stratified to stress-test the rubric boundaries and the normalization table, not randomly drawn:

| Stratum | n | Purpose | Example Profile_IDs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Breadth = 0 (zero-evidence) | 3 | Tests the two zero-evidence sub-cases in `ALCSM_Coding_Rulebook_v1.1_Addendum.md` §5 (no AI-related experience vs. AI-related experience with unusable industry) | LP0041, LP0170, LP0182 |
| Breadth = 1 (low) | 3 | Baseline agreement on the largest single band (47.8% of the population) | LP0001, LP0009, LP0010 |
| Breadth = 2 | 3 | Second-largest band (31.0%); boundary with band 1 and band 3 | LP0003, LP0011, LP0015 |
| Breadth = 3 (mid/boundary) | 3 | Includes at least one profile where the normalization table's merges are actually invoked | LP0004, LP0005, LP0007 |
| Breadth = 4 (high) | 2 | Includes a profile also touching a deliberately-unmerged ambiguous industry label, deliberately overlapping stress goals | LP0006, LP0038 |
| Breadth = 5 (ceiling) | 2 | Tests the top-band boundary and its thin population (only 5 such profiles exist in the full dataset) | LP0002, LP0083 |
| Ambiguous-label-specific | 2 | Profiles touching a deliberately-unmerged raw value (`E-commerce and Retail`, `Travel Technology`, `Chemicals`, `Professional Association`) not otherwise captured above | LP0036, LP0040 |
| **Total** | **18** | | |

Profile_IDs are the dataset's own pseudonymous identifiers (no name, URL, or other direct identifier); listing them here does not disclose personal data and follows the same convention already used throughout this project's governance documents.

**Raters:** two independent raters (Rater A / Rater B, consistent with `ALCSM_Coding_Rulebook_and_Pilot_Design.md` Part 2 roles), trained on `ALCSM_Coding_Rulebook_v1.1_Addendum.md` before coding, using 2 non-pilot practice profiles for calibration exactly as the parent rulebook's §2.3 workflow specifies (practice round excluded from reliability calculation).

**Procedure:** identical to the parent rulebook's Part 2 workflow — independent coding with no cross-communication, blind cross-check only after both logs are complete, tie-breaking per the parent rulebook's §1.5 escalation ladder plus the D6/D5 boundary rule in the addendum's §9, reliability computed on pre-adjudication scores only.

**Reliability statistic and thresholds:** Krippendorff's alpha, computed for D6 alone (not pooled with D1–D5), using the same thresholds already established project-wide (`ALCSM_Coding_Rulebook_and_Pilot_Design.md` §2.4–§2.5): α ≥ .80 → proceed as-worded; .667 ≤ α < .80 → acceptable provisionally, review disagreement cases, minor wording clarification permitted; α < .667 → do not proceed, revise rulebook wording (never the frozen scoring criteria) and re-pilot. Ambiguity/contradiction flag rate >30% of pilot profiles is treated as a rubric-clarity problem independent of the alpha value, per the same existing decision rule.

**Specific things the disagreement analysis must check, beyond the generic per-dimension review:** (a) whether disagreement clusters on the §6/normalization-table judgment (annotators treating a deliberately-unmerged pair inconsistently) rather than on the band boundary itself — the addendum's worked Example 4 exists specifically to test this; (b) whether the single D5/D6 boundary case (§5 above) is resolvable using the proposed tie-breaking rule or needs revision; (c) whether the zero-evidence sub-case distinction (`present`/score 0 vs. `unavailable`) is being applied consistently, given it is a new distinction for this dimension.

**Priori expectation, stated before running the pilot** (consistent with the parent rulebook's §2.5 recommendation to pre-register expectations): D6 v1.1 is expected to achieve **higher** reliability than the original D6 would have, because it depends on a structured field rather than self-presentation/behavioral inference — if the pilot instead shows reliability at or below where D5/D6 sat under v1.0, that is itself a signal the normalization table or the deliberately-unmerged cases need revision before proceeding, not evidence the replacement strategy was wrong.

## 8. Risks (consolidated from `ALCSM_D6_Industry_Breadth_v1.1.md` §12)

1. Employer-labeling inconsistency in the raw `Industry` field (mitigated, not eliminated, by the normalization table).
2. Breadth reflects an observable pattern, not intentional career strategy — could result from involuntary job changes or consulting-style multi-client structures as easily as deliberate cross-industry positioning.
3. Thin top band (n=5, 1.0%) limits discriminating power at the high end.
4. Concentration in bands 1–2 (78.8%) limits discriminating power overall; most of the dimension's information is in distinguishing "one" from "two or more."
5. Weak-to-moderate correlation with an AI-tenure proxy means D6 is not fully independent variation from D3 — disclosed, not disqualifying, for a formative index.
6. Near-total absence of D6-relevant language on the job-posting side (§5) means Gap₆ (the credential-gap statistic for this dimension) will be computed from an extremely sparse sample — must be reported as a substantive finding, not smoothed into the same confidence band as Gap₁–Gap₅.

## 9. MSc-level scope note

The normalization table, rubric, and distinctiveness check are all deliberately kept at thesis-appropriate scope (small manually-reasoned tables, direct count-to-band mapping, structural proxies rather than a bespoke validation study) — see `ALCSM_D6_Industry_Breadth_v1.1.md` §13. This is a researcher-constructed operationalization, not an externally validated scale, and is documented as such throughout.
